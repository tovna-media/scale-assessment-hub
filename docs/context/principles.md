# Principles

The "why" behind the golden rules in CLAUDE.md. When a design or copy call is ambiguous, decide toward these.

## Coach in the room, without the coach
The product exists because Rich can't personally coach everyone. The app carries three things Rich carries in person: the why (what a task is for and what you get from it), the guidance (how to do it), and the rhythm (daily and weekly nudges). A screen that just collects input without doing those three things is off-brand. Favor context, encouragement, and reminders over bare forms.

## Sell the outcome, and only after the gap lands
People don't want more assessments or reports. They want to stop feeling stuck. The gap report is when they feel it, so that's when the upgrade story belongs. Before the gap report a member's only job is to finish the assessments — don't distract them with upgrade pressure. After it, sell the coaching system as the way to close the gaps they just saw. Copy is about results and being coached, never "homework," "worksheets," or "unlock more assessments."

## Honest measurement
The app cannot observe real leadership behavior. Habit checks, action completions, and streaks are the member telling us they did it. Useful, but never presented as verified. Due dates let us show due / done / still-open; they are not proof. Never build a metric or label implying the app knows what the member actually did.

## The AI coach is a real coach's voice, not a data oracle
The Fully Resourced AI Coach is an external CoachVox avatar trained on Rich's material. It does not read the member's scores or gap report. Its value is that it sounds like Rich, which generic ChatGPT and a static course can't. Market that truth; never imply it knows the member's specific gaps.

## Calm over clutter
A leadership tool for busy people. Default to fewer elements, more white space, one clear next action per screen. When in doubt, cut. "Too cluttered" is the most common way these screens go wrong.
